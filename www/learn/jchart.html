jChart — the diagram workbench

Why it exists

Mermaid is a great language for an LLM to write and a poor medium for a human to edit. jChart uses each for what it's good at: Claude drafts the structure in Mermaid, the app converts it to an Excalidraw scene, and from that point on the canvas — not the Mermaid — is the source of truth. Drag boxes, reroute arrows, redraw parts freehand; nothing gets clobbered by a regenerate.

Feedback is spatial too: instead of describing a shape in chat (“the second box from the left…”), you pin a note directly to it. Notes land in a .notes.json sidecar next to the scene, and Claude reads both to revise.

The diagram loop

  1. Ask for a diagram. The j-chart skill has Claude draft it in Mermaid and import it as a scene.
  2. Edit on the canvas. Open it in jChart — move, restyle, redraw, delete. It's a full Excalidraw editor.
  3. Annotate shapes. Pin notes to exactly the elements you mean.
  4. Claude revises. It reads the scene and your notes from .data/jchart/ and updates the diagram without discarding your edits.

Skills that drive it

j-chart

Turns a request into a diagram, opens it in jChart as an editable Excalidraw canvas, and gets your edits and per-shape notes back. The whole loop — draft, import, read-back — is this one skill.

The shared chart pool

jChart's scenes are not private to jChart. The @jsuite/charting package — a Nuxt layer with the Excalidraw canvas, Mermaid→scene conversion, and a /api/charts store over .data/jchart/ — is extended by every consumer app. That means a chart embedded in a jExplain article is the very same object jChart opens: edits and notes flow both ways, and jChart stays the specialised workbench UI on top of the pool.
